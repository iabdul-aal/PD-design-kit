\chapter{Germanium-on-Silicon PIN Photodetector: Electromagnetic Foundations,
         Device Physics, and Multi-Physics Simulation}
\label{ch:photodetector}

The detection of optical signals at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre} constitutes the
first irreversible transduction step in any silicon-photonic receiver chain:
the continuous optical field is collapsed into a discrete electrical current,
and whatever information is lost at this stage propagates as an irrecoverable
noise penalty into every subsequent digital signal processing block. Understanding
this step at the level of first principles is therefore not merely a theoretical
exercise but a practical necessity for the coherent design of the full transceiver
system developed in the companion chapters of this thesis.

This chapter develops that understanding from the ground up. The first section
constructs the analytical framework rigorously, beginning with Maxwell's equations
in a dispersive absorbing medium, from which the absorption coefficient of
germanium emerges as a derived quantity rather than an empirically asserted one.
Carrier transport, recombination, and the full self-consistent drift-diffusion
system are then formalised in both their transient and steady-state forms, and
all performance metrics are defined with sufficient precision to serve as
unambiguous comparison axes throughout the chapter. The second section applies
those metrics to a critical survey of the experimental literature, identifying
the physical mechanisms that have driven the successive bandwidth improvements
in Ge-on-Si photodetectors over the past fifteen years. The third section
presents the device architecture and the coupled optical-electrical simulation
methodology. The fourth section presents the simulation results as a validation
of the physical models introduced in the first section rather than as a catalogue
of outputs. A Discussion section then interprets each result through the lens of
competing physical mechanisms and scaling arguments. A Device-to-System Projection
section derives, strictly from the extracted device metrics, the implications for
the receiver sensitivity of the full optical link, providing the controlled top-down
bridge to the DSP chapters that follow. A Conclusion section distils the principal
physical insights and identifies the specific open questions that future experimental
work must address.

\subsection{Electromagnetic Propagation in an Absorbing Dispersive Medium}
\label{subsec:pd_maxwell}

The starting point for all optical analysis in this chapter is the macroscopic
form of Maxwell's equations in a non-magnetic, source-free medium. In SI units
and in the frequency domain, obtained by assuming a time dependence
$e^{-i\omega t}$ and taking the Fourier transform, these read:
\begin{align}
    \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r},\omega)
        &= i\omega\mu_0\,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{r},\omega),
        \label{eq:maxwell_curl_e}\\
    \nabla \times \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{r},\omega)
        &= -i\omega\tilde{\varepsilon}(\omega)\,\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r},\omega),
        \label{eq:maxwell_curl_h}
\end{align}
where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space and $\tilde{\varepsilon}(\omega)$
is the complex permittivity of the medium. Taking the curl of
\cref{eq:maxwell_curl_e} and substituting \cref{eq:maxwell_curl_h} in the
absence of free charges ($\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = 0$) yields the
Helmholtz wave equation:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r},\omega)
    + \tilde{k}^2(\omega)\,\tilde{\mathbf{E}}(\mathbf{r},\omega) = \mathbf{0},
    \label{eq:helmholtz}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{k}(\omega) = \omega\sqrt{\mu_0\tilde{\varepsilon}(\omega)}$ is the
complex wavenumber. It is conventional to express $\tilde{k}$ through the
\emph{complex refractive index}:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{n}(\omega) \equiv \frac{c\,\tilde{k}(\omega)}{\omega}
    = n(\omega) + i\kappa(\omega),
    \label{eq:complex_n}
\end{equation}
where $n(\omega)$ is the real (phase) refractive index and $\kappa(\omega) \geq 0$
is the extinction coefficient, both being real functions of frequency. The plane-wave
solution to \cref{eq:helmholtz} propagating along the $+\hat{z}$ direction is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{E}}(z,\omega)
    = \mathbf{E}_0\exp\!\Bigl[i\tilde{n}(\omega)\frac{\omega z}{c}\Bigr]
    = \mathbf{E}_0
      \exp\!\Bigl[i\,n(\omega)\frac{\omega z}{c}\Bigr]
      \exp\!\Bigl[-\kappa(\omega)\frac{\omega z}{c}\Bigr].
    \label{eq:plane_wave}
\end{equation}
The first exponential in \cref{eq:plane_wave} describes phase accumulation at
the real phase velocity $c/n$; the second describes spatial attenuation. The
time-averaged Poynting intensity $I(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}[\tilde{\mathbf{E}}
\times \tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*]\cdot\hat{z}$ scales as $|\tilde{\mathbf{E}}|^2$
and therefore decays as $\exp[-2\kappa\omega z/c]$. Identifying the exponential
decay constant with the phenomenological Beer-Lambert coefficient immediately
yields the fundamental relation between $\kappa$ and $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(\omega) = \frac{2\omega\kappa(\omega)}{c}
    = \frac{4\pi\kappa(\lambda)}{\lambda},
    \label{eq:alpha_kappa}
\end{equation}
so that the Beer-Lambert law for the guided intensity,
\begin{equation}
    I(z) = I_0\exp\!\bigl[-\alpha(\lambda)\,z\bigr],
    \label{eq:beer_lambert}
\end{equation}
emerges as a corollary of the plane-wave solution to \cref{eq:helmholtz} rather
than an independent empirical postulate. The absorbed power density per unit volume
follows directly from the negative spatial gradient of the intensity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{r})
    = -\frac{\partial I}{\partial z}
    = \alpha(\lambda)\,I(\mathbf{r}).
    \label{eq:abs_density}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Band Structure of Germanium and the Direct-Gap Absorption Mechanism}
\label{subsec:pd_bandstructure}

Germanium is an indirect-gap semiconductor whose lowest conduction-band minimum
lies at the $L$-point of the Brillouin zone, separated from the valence-band
maximum at $\Gamma$ by an indirect gap
$E_{g,\mathrm{ind}} = \SI{0.66}{\electronvolt}$ at \SI{300}{\kelvin}. A secondary
conduction-band minimum at the $\Gamma$-point defines a direct gap
$E_{g,\mathrm{dir}} = \SI{0.80}{\electronvolt}$~\cite{AshcroftMermin2021,Rossler2009,Ziman1972}.
The photon energy at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre} is
$\hbar\omega = hc/\lambda \approx \SI{0.946}{\electronvolt}$, which exceeds
$E_{g,\mathrm{dir}}$ by approximately \SI{0.15}{\electronvolt}. Optical absorption
at this wavelength therefore proceeds predominantly through \emph{direct} interband
transitions at the $\Gamma$-point, for which crystal momentum is intrinsically
conserved without phonon assistance~\cite{Basu2003,Kantorovich2004}. This is the
physical origin of the large extinction coefficient $\kappa(\SI{1310}{\nano\metre})$
in \cref{eq:alpha_kappa} and the consequently large $\alpha$ of order
\SI{e3}{\per\centi\metre}. Silicon, whose lowest direct transition at
\SI{3.4}{\electronvolt} is far above \SI{0.946}{\electronvolt} and whose
indirect gap of \SI{1.12}{\electronvolt} makes it effectively transparent at O-band
wavelengths, cannot be used as the absorber. This single material-physics argument
is the entire justification for the Ge-on-Si heterostructure that forms the subject
of this chapter.

Integrating \cref{eq:abs_density} through an absorber of length $L$ gives the
fraction of incident power converted to heat (and thence to electron-hole pairs)
in a single pass:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}(L) = 1 - \exp\!\bigl[-\alpha(\lambda)\,L\bigr].
    \label{eq:abs_fraction}
\end{equation}
The quantity $\mathcal{A}$ is the primary optical design variable: for a fixed
material $\alpha$, shortening $L$ reduces $\mathcal{A}$ and therefore reduces
responsivity, while unnecessarily long absorbers increase device capacitance
without further improving $\mathcal{A}$. The saturation of $\mathcal{A}$ with
respect to $L$, studied numerically in \cref{subsec:pd_optical_results},
determines the optimal absorber length.

\subsection{Carrier Generation and Recombination}
\label{subsec:pd_generation}

Each photon absorbed within the germanium volume promotes one electron from the
valence band to the conduction band, producing one free electron-hole pair.
The volumetric optical generation rate—the number of such pairs produced per unit
volume per unit time—is obtained by dividing the local absorbed power density of
\cref{eq:abs_density} by the photon energy $\hbar\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r})
    = \frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{r})}{\hbar\omega}
    = \frac{\alpha(\lambda)\,I(\mathbf{r})}{\hbar\omega}
    \quad [\si{\per\centi\metre\cubed\per\second}].
    \label{eq:gen_rate}
\end{equation}
This profile, which mirrors the spatial intensity distribution of
\cref{eq:beer_lambert}, enters the carrier transport equations as the
inhomogeneous source term responsible for photocurrent. Its spatial
non-uniformity—highest at the taper exit and decaying exponentially in the
propagation direction—has a direct consequence for the relative contributions
of drift and diffusion to carrier collection efficiency, as discussed in
\cref{sec:pd_discussion}.

Photogenerated carriers are subject to recombination before they can be swept out
of the junction. Three mechanisms are quantitatively relevant in germanium
photodiodes operating under moderate reverse bias~\cite{SzeNg2006,Basu2003}.
\emph{Shockley-Read-Hall} (SRH) recombination, mediated by deep-level trap states
with energy $E_t$ within the forbidden gap, is described by the Hall-Shockley-Read
expression:
\begin{equation}
    U_{\mathrm{SRH}}
    = \frac{np - n_i^2}{\tau_p\!\left(n + n_1\right)
      + \tau_n\!\left(p + p_1\right)},
    \label{eq:srh}
\end{equation}
where $n$ and $p$ are the local electron and hole concentrations, $n_i$ is the
intrinsic carrier concentration, $\tau_n$ and $\tau_p$ are the minority-carrier
SRH lifetimes, and $n_1 = n_i\exp[(E_t - E_i)/kT]$,
$p_1 = n_i\exp[(E_i - E_t)/kT]$ are the Fermi statistics-weighted trap occupation
densities. Within the fully depleted intrinsic region of a reverse-biased
junction, where $n \approx p \approx n_i \ll n_1, p_1$ is not generally valid;
rather, at the midgap trap condition $E_t = E_i$, \cref{eq:srh} reduces to the
generation-dominated limit:
\begin{equation}
    U_{\mathrm{SRH,dep}} \approx \frac{n_i}{\tau_n + \tau_p}
    \equiv \frac{n_i}{2\tau_0},
    \label{eq:srh_dep}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0 = (\tau_n + \tau_p)/2$ is the effective generation lifetime. This
term dominates the dark current of Ge photodiodes: germanium's intrinsic carrier
concentration $n_i \approx \SI{2.4e13}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ at \SI{300}{\kelvin}
exceeds that of silicon ($\SI{1.5e10}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$) by more than three
orders of magnitude, making the SRH generation current intrinsically larger at
identical junction geometry and reverse bias. \emph{Auger} recombination, relevant
primarily in the heavily doped contact layers, follows
$U_{\mathrm{Auger}} = C_n n(n p - n_i^2) + C_p p(np - n_i^2)$,
where $C_n$ and $C_p$ are the Auger coefficients for electron-initiated and
hole-initiated processes respectively. \emph{Radiative} band-to-band
recombination contributes negligibly at the doping levels and carrier densities
considered here, because germanium is an indirect-gap material and the direct-gap
radiative rate is suppressed by the large density of indirect-gap final states.

\subsection{Drift-Diffusion Transport: Transient and Steady-State Formulations}
\label{subsec:pd_transport}

The self-consistent distribution of carriers and the electrostatic field within
the device are governed by the coupled Poisson and drift-diffusion
equations~\cite{SzeNg2006,Yariv1997}. Poisson's equation relates the electrostatic
potential $\phi(\mathbf{r},t)$ to the net charge density:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla\cdot\!\left[\varepsilon(\mathbf{r})\,\nabla\phi(\mathbf{r},t)\right]
    = -q\!\left[p(\mathbf{r},t) - n(\mathbf{r},t)
               + N_D^+(\mathbf{r}) - N_A^-(\mathbf{r})\right],
    \label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon(\mathbf{r})$ is the position-dependent permittivity, $q$ is the
elementary charge, and $N_D^+$, $N_A^-$ are the ionised donor and acceptor
concentrations. The electric field is $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t) = -\nabla\phi$.
The electron and hole current density vectors in the drift-diffusion approximation
are:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{J}_n(\mathbf{r},t)
        &= q\mu_n(\mathbf{r})\,n(\mathbf{r},t)\,\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)
           + qD_n(\mathbf{r})\,\nabla n(\mathbf{r},t),
        \label{eq:Jn}\\
    \mathbf{J}_p(\mathbf{r},t)
        &= q\mu_p(\mathbf{r})\,p(\mathbf{r},t)\,\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)
           - qD_p(\mathbf{r})\,\nabla p(\mathbf{r},t),
        \label{eq:Jp}
\end{align}
where $\mu_n$, $\mu_p$ are the field-dependent carrier mobilities and
$D_n = \mu_n kT/q$, $D_p = \mu_p kT/q$ are the diffusion coefficients, related
to the mobilities through the Einstein relation for non-degenerate statistics.
The first terms of \cref{eq:Jn,eq:Jp} represent drift under the electric field;
the second terms represent diffusion driven by concentration gradients. The
general, time-dependent carrier continuity equations, which balance generation,
recombination, and the divergence of the current flux, are:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial n(\mathbf{r},t)}{\partial t}
        &= \frac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_n(\mathbf{r},t)
           - U_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathbf{r},t) + G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r},t),
        \label{eq:cont_n_transient}\\
    \frac{\partial p(\mathbf{r},t)}{\partial t}
        &= -\frac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_p(\mathbf{r},t)
           - U_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathbf{r},t) + G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r},t),
        \label{eq:cont_p_transient}
\end{align}
where $U_{\mathrm{net}} = U_{\mathrm{SRH}} + U_{\mathrm{Auger}} + U_{\mathrm{rad}}$
is the total net recombination rate. The transient form of
\cref{eq:cont_n_transient,eq:cont_p_transient} is the natural starting point for
bandwidth analysis: it is the time-domain response of the carrier system to an
impulsive optical stimulus $G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r},t) = G_0(\mathbf{r})\delta(t)$
that, upon Fourier transformation, yields the frequency-dependent photocurrent
transfer function from which $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ is extracted. Under
\emph{steady-state} continuous-wave illumination, $\partial/\partial t \equiv 0$
and \cref{eq:cont_n_transient,eq:cont_p_transient} reduce to:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_n
        &= U_{\mathrm{net}} - G_{\mathrm{opt}},
        \label{eq:cont_n}\\
    \frac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}_p
        &= -\!\left(U_{\mathrm{net}} - G_{\mathrm{opt}}\right).
        \label{eq:cont_p}
\end{align}
The complete self-consistent system—\cref{eq:poisson} through \cref{eq:cont_p}—is
the foundational PDE set solved numerically in both the commercial finite-element
solver and the Physics-Informed Neural Network framework described in
\cref{sec:pd_design}.

\subsection{Performance Metrics: Rigorous Definitions and Physical Interpretation}
\label{subsec:pd_metrics}

\subsubsection{Responsivity and Quantum Efficiency}
\label{subsubsec:pd_responsivity}

The \emph{external responsivity} $\mathcal{R}$ is the ratio of the collected
photocurrent to the incident optical power:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R} \equiv \frac{I_{\mathrm{ph}}}{P_{\mathrm{opt}}}
    \quad [\si{\ampere\per\watt}],
    \label{eq:responsivity}
\end{equation}
where $I_{\mathrm{ph}} = I_{\mathrm{ill}} - I_d$ is extracted from the difference
between illuminated and dark current-voltage characteristics. The theoretical upper
bound, obtained by setting the collected current equal to $q$ times the incident
photon flux $P_{\mathrm{opt}}/\hbar\omega$, is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\max} = \frac{q\lambda}{hc},
    \label{eq:rmax}
\end{equation}
which evaluates to $\mathcal{R}_{\max} \approx \SI{1.057}{\ampere\per\watt}$
at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre}. The \emph{external quantum efficiency} (EQE)
$\eta_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is the ratio of collected electron-hole pairs per second to
incident photons per second, and is related to $\mathcal{R}$ by:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{ext}}
    = \frac{I_{\mathrm{ph}}/q}{P_{\mathrm{opt}}/\hbar\omega}
    = \mathcal{R}\cdot\frac{hc}{q\lambda}.
    \label{eq:eqe}
\end{equation}
The \emph{internal quantum efficiency} (IQE) $\eta_{\mathrm{int}}$ measures
carrier collection efficiency relative only to photons actually absorbed within
the active volume, thereby isolating the electrical performance from optical
losses:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{int}}
    = \frac{I_{\mathrm{ph}}/q}{P_{\mathrm{abs}}/\hbar\omega},
    \label{eq:iqe}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathrm{abs}} = \int_\Omega \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}\,\mathrm{d}V$
is integrated over the absorber volume $\Omega$. The three efficiency measures
are linked through the waveguide-to-absorber coupling efficiency $\eta_c$ and
the single-pass absorption fraction $\mathcal{A}$ of \cref{eq:abs_fraction}:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{ext}}
    = \eta_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot\eta_c\cdot\mathcal{A}
    = \eta_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot\eta_c\cdot
      \!\left(1 - e^{-\alpha(\lambda) L}\right).
    \label{eq:qe_chain}
\end{equation}
A device with $\eta_{\mathrm{int}} \approx 1$ (negligible recombination loss within
the absorber) will still exhibit $\mathcal{R} < \mathcal{R}_{\max}$ if $\mathcal{A}
< 1$, confirming that the absorber length $L$ is the primary responsivity design
lever through the Beer-Lambert factor of \cref{eq:qe_chain}.

\subsubsection{Dark Current}
\label{subsubsec:pd_dark}

Dark current $I_d$ flows through the reverse-biased junction in the absence of
optical excitation and constitutes the irreducible noise floor of the device.
Three generation-recombination mechanisms contribute~\cite{SzeNg2006}. The
\emph{SRH generation} current density within the depleted intrinsic layer of
thickness $W_i$ follows directly from \cref{eq:srh_dep}:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{SRH}} = q\,U_{\mathrm{SRH,dep}}\,W_i
    = \frac{q\,n_i\,W_i}{2\tau_0},
    \label{eq:jdark_srh}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0 = (\tau_n + \tau_p)/2$ is the effective generation lifetime.
\emph{Minority-carrier diffusion} from the quasi-neutral contact regions contributes:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{diff}}
    = \frac{qD_p\,n_i^2}{L_p\,N_D} + \frac{qD_n\,n_i^2}{L_n\,N_A},
    \label{eq:jdark_diff}
\end{equation}
where $L_p = \sqrt{D_p\tau_p}$, $L_n = \sqrt{D_n\tau_n}$ are the minority-carrier
diffusion lengths in the $n^{++}$-Ge and $p^{++}$-Si regions respectively, and
$N_D$, $N_A$ are the corresponding doping concentrations. The $n_i^2$ dependence
of \cref{eq:jdark_diff} is strongly suppressed by the heavy doping of the contact
layers, which reduces the minority carrier equilibrium densities
$p_{n0} = n_i^2/N_D$ and $n_{p0} = n_i^2/N_A$ to negligible values. \emph{Interface
recombination} at the Ge/Si heterojunction, modelled through the interface
recombination velocity $v_s$~\cite{SzeNg2006}, contributes:
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{surf}} \approx \frac{q\,n_i\,v_s}{2},
    \label{eq:jdark_surf}
\end{equation}
where $v_s$ is defined as the proportionality constant between the interface
recombination rate and $n_i/2$ under the depletion condition. The total dark
current is:
\begin{equation}
    I_d = A_j\!\left(J_{\mathrm{SRH}} + J_{\mathrm{diff}}
          + J_{\mathrm{surf}}\right),
    \label{eq:idark_total}
\end{equation}
where $A_j$ is the junction area. The contrast between the linear $n_i$ dependence
in \cref{eq:jdark_srh,eq:jdark_surf} and the $n_i^2$ dependence in
\cref{eq:jdark_diff} reflects the differing microscopic origins of each mechanism
and explains why SRH generation dominates in reverse-biased Ge junctions at room
temperature, where $n_i^2/N_{D,A} \ll n_i$.

\subsubsection{Electrical Bandwidth}
\label{subsubsec:pd_bw}

The frequency response of the photodiode is limited by two physically distinct
mechanisms. Defining their respective 3\,dB corner frequencies $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$
(transit time) and $f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ (circuit), the combined bandwidth under the
small-signal approximation and assuming independent roll-off contributions is:
\begin{equation}
    f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}
    = \left(\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{tt}}^2}
      + \frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{RC}}^2}\right)^{-1/2}.
    \label{eq:bw_total}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Transit-time limit.}
The transit-time limitation arises from the finite time required for
photogenerated carriers to drift across the intrinsic region of thickness $W_i$.
When the reverse bias is sufficient for carrier velocities to saturate, the
electron and hole drift velocities reach their respective saturation values
$v_{\mathrm{d,sat},e}$ and $v_{\mathrm{d,sat},h}$. In germanium,
$v_{\mathrm{d,sat},h} \approx \SI{4e6}{\centi\metre\per\second}$ and
$v_{\mathrm{d,sat},e} \approx \SI{6e6}{\centi\metre\per\second}$; the slower holes
set the transit-time limit. The impulse response of the device current to an
infinitely short optical pulse — the \emph{current waveform} — is a rectangular
pulse of duration $\tau_{\mathrm{tr}} = W_i/v_{\mathrm{d,sat},h}$, because all
carriers generated at arbitrary positions $0 \leq z \leq W_i$ contribute current
for a time proportional to their remaining distance to the collecting electrode.
Fourier analysis of this trapezoidal impulse response yields a transit-time-limited
3\,dB bandwidth of:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{tt}} = \frac{0.45\,v_{\mathrm{d,sat},h}}{W_i},
    \label{eq:bw_tt}
\end{equation}
where the coefficient $0.45$ arises from the sinc-like spectral roll-off of the
rectangular current pulse. For $W_i = \SI{350}{\nano\metre}$, this evaluates to
$f_{\mathrm{tt}} \approx \SI{51}{\giga\hertz}$.

\paragraph{RC limit.}
The junction capacitance of the fully depleted intrinsic region is modelled as a
parallel-plate capacitor:
\begin{equation}
    C_j = \frac{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Ge}}\,A_j}{W_i},
    \label{eq:cj}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Ge}} \approx 16.2$ is the static relative permittivity
of germanium and $A_j = W_m \times L$ is the junction area, with $W_m$ and $L$
the mesa width and length respectively. In practice, parasitic contributions from
bondpads and interconnect routing augment this to
$C_{\mathrm{tot}} = C_j + C_{\mathrm{par}}$. The load and series resistance combine
as $R_{\mathrm{tot}} = R_s + R_L$, where $R_L = \SI{50}{\ohm}$ is the standard
termination. The RC-limited bandwidth is:
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{RC}} = \frac{1}{2\pi R_{\mathrm{tot}}\,C_j}.
    \label{eq:bw_rc}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:bw_tt} and \eqref{eq:cj} expose a structural constraint of
the vertical architecture: both $f_{\mathrm{tt}} \propto W_i^{-1}$ and
$C_j \propto W_i^{-1}$ (hence $f_{\mathrm{RC}} \propto W_i$) scale as inverse or
linear functions of $W_i$, so that the product
$f_{\mathrm{tt}}\cdot f_{\mathrm{RC}} \propto W_i^{-1}\cdot W_i = \mathrm{const}$
is independent of $W_i$. The total bandwidth from \cref{eq:bw_total} therefore
cannot be improved by varying $W_i$ alone \emph{once the two contributions are
comparable in magnitude}, because any reduction in $W_i$ that increases
$f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ simultaneously decreases $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$ by the same factor.
If the device is strongly RC-limited ($f_{\mathrm{RC}} \ll f_{\mathrm{tt}}$),
increasing $W_i$ does temporarily improve $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ by allowing $C_j$
to fall faster than $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$ degrades; however, this regime is quickly
exhausted once the two terms balance. The only path to higher $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$
at \emph{fixed} $W_i$ is to reduce $R_s$ independently of the geometry — the
principal motivation for the U-shaped electrode architecture discussed in
\cref{subsec:pd_architecture}.

\subsubsection{Capacitance and Series Resistance}
\label{subsubsec:pd_cres}

The series resistance $R_s$ in the vertical n-i-p architecture is dominated by the
anode current path through the $p^{++}$-Si platform between the metal anode contact
and the base of the germanium mesa sidewall. For a sheet resistance $R_{\square}$
and a lateral conduction path of mean length $\ell_a$ and width $w_a$:
\begin{equation}
    R_{s,\mathrm{anode}} \approx R_\square\,\frac{\ell_a}{w_a}
    = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{Si}}}{t_{\mathrm{Si}}}\cdot\frac{\ell_a}{w_a},
    \label{eq:rs_anode}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{Si}}$ is the resistivity of the $p^{++}$-Si platform and
$t_{\mathrm{Si}} = \SI{220}{\nano\metre}$ is its thickness. The primary figure of
merit for electrode geometry optimisation is therefore the RC time constant:
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{\mathrm{RC}} = R_s\,C_j,
    \label{eq:tau_rc}
\end{equation}
from which $f_{\mathrm{RC}} = (2\pi\tau_{\mathrm{RC}})^{-1}$. Reducing $\ell_a$
by bringing the anode metal closer to the mesa base—realised through the U-shaped
electrode geometry of \cref{subsec:pd_architecture}—directly reduces $R_s$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{RC}}$ without modifying $C_j$, $W_i$, or any optical parameter.

\subsubsection{Noise Spectral Densities and Noise-Equivalent Power}
\label{subsubsec:pd_noise}

Two noise processes are fundamental to any PIN photodiode~\cite{SalehTeich1991,Yariv1997}.
\emph{Shot noise} arises from the quantum-mechanical discreteness of charge carriers
at the output terminal. For a stationary Poisson arrival process, the one-sided
power spectral density of the shot noise current is:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{shot}}(f) = 2q\!\left(I_d + I_{\mathrm{ph}}\right)
    \quad [\si{\ampere\squared\per\hertz}],
    \label{eq:shot}
\end{equation}
flat across all frequencies up to $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$. The dark current
contribution $2qI_d$ is irreducible: it persists even in the absence of any optical
signal and places a lower bound on the detector noise floor that is independent of
the optical power level. \emph{Johnson (thermal) noise} from the load resistance
$R_L$ contributes:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{thermal}}(f) = \frac{4kT}{R_L}
    \quad [\si{\ampere\squared\per\hertz}],
    \label{eq:thermal}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant and $T = \SI{300}{\kelvin}$. The
\emph{noise-equivalent power} (NEP), defined as the optical power at which the
electrical signal-to-noise ratio equals unity in a \SI{1}{\hertz} detection
bandwidth, is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{NEP} = \frac{\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{shot}} + S_{\mathrm{thermal}}}}{\mathcal{R}}
    \quad [\si{\watt\per\sqrt\hertz}].
    \label{eq:nep}
\end{equation}
The \emph{specific detectivity} $D^* = \sqrt{A_j}/\mathrm{NEP}$, with units
$\si{\centi\metre\,\hertz^{1/2}\,\watt^{-1}}$ (Jones), normalises the NEP to the
square root of the detector area and thereby enables comparison across devices of
different footprint. Under the dark-current-limited (shot-noise-dominated) regime,
where $S_{\mathrm{shot}}\approx 2qI_d$ and $S_{\mathrm{thermal}}$ is negligible,
$D^*$ becomes:
\begin{equation}
    D^*_{\mathrm{shot}} = \frac{\mathcal{R}\sqrt{A_j}}{\sqrt{2qI_d}},
    \label{eq:dstar}
\end{equation}
an expression that connects responsivity, dark current, and device area into a
single figure of merit consistent with \cref{tab:results}.

\subsection{Evolution of Waveguide-Integrated Ge-on-Si Photodetectors}
\label{subsec:pd_history}

The Ge-on-Si waveguide photodetector field has evolved over approximately two
decades through successive generations of electrode and junction engineering,
each generation interpretable through the metric framework of
\cref{sec:pd_concept}. What follows is not a descriptive catalogue but a causal
narrative: each development is evaluated in terms of which specific term in
\cref{eq:bw_total,eq:idark_total,eq:qe_chain} it addressed and why the residual
performance gap persisted.

The landmark demonstration of Vivien~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Vivien2009}
established a silicon-on-insulator (SOI) waveguide-coupled Ge p-i-n detector with
$\mathcal{R} \approx \SI{1.0}{\ampere\per\watt}$ and
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{42}{\giga\hertz}$ at the O-band, confirming that the
direct-gap absorption mechanism of \cref{subsec:pd_bandstructure} translates into
competitive responsivity on the silicon platform. The \SI{42}{\giga\hertz} result
was RC-limited, as the electrode geometry left $R_s$ at a value that caused
$f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ of \cref{eq:bw_rc} to dominate over $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$.
Ang~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Ang2010} subsequently demonstrated evanescently coupled
Ge-on-SOI fabricated at sufficiently low thermal budget to remain CMOS-compatible,
establishing process integration as an additional hard constraint that the device
architecture must satisfy independently of the performance optimisation.

The mid-2010s were characterised by systematic reductions of $R_s$ through
contact geometry engineering. Chen~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Chen2015} demonstrated a
silicon-contacted Ge p-i-n delivering $\mathcal{R} = \SI{0.74}{\ampere\per\watt}$
and $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{35}{\giga\hertz}$; the responsivity shortfall
relative to the quantum-efficiency limit of \cref{eq:rmax} was attributed to
incomplete absorption at the selected absorber length and residual coupling
loss through $\eta_c$ in \cref{eq:qe_chain}. The same group subsequently achieved
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{67}{\giga\hertz}$ at only \SI{1}{\volt} reverse bias
through electrode repositioning~\cite{Chen2016Si}, reducing $\ell_a$ in
\cref{eq:rs_anode} and thereby $R_s$, which in turn raised $f_{\mathrm{RC}}$
per \cref{eq:bw_rc} until it no longer dominated \cref{eq:bw_total}. Novack
\textit{et al.}~\cite{Novack2013} achieved $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{60}{\giga\hertz}$
by a different route: the introduction of wire-bond inductance as an
equivalent-circuit peaking element, which modifies the effective load impedance
and extends the 3\,dB point of the electrical transfer function beyond the passive
RC pole of \cref{eq:bw_rc} without any modification of the junction geometry.

The lateral junction architecture introduced by Virot~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Virot2017}
decouples the optical mode depth from the depletion region width: by forming the
junction horizontally between $p$-doped and $n$-doped silicon regions flanking an
intrinsic germanium strip, the effective capacitance-controlling dimension is the
horizontal intrinsic width rather than the absorber thickness. This provides an
additional degree of freedom for $C_j$ minimisation in \cref{eq:cj} that is
geometrically independent of $\mathcal{A}$ in \cref{eq:abs_fraction}, yielding
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{67}{\giga\hertz}$ with dark currents below \SI{10}{\nano\ampere}.

Parasitic engineering of the vertical architecture was pushed to its prior limit
by Shi~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Shi2021}, who demonstrated
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{80}{\giga\hertz}$ through systematic minimisation of
parasitic pad capacitance and optimised electrode routing. The bandwidth ceiling
for the lateral architecture was subsequently located by Lischke~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Lischke2021},
who reported $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{265}{\giga\hertz}$ in a device with
aggressively scaled junction area, at the cost of severely reduced responsivity and
substantially more complex fabrication. The benchmark for the present work is the
$\SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$ vertical Ge-on-Si photodiode of Shi~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Shi2024},
which surpasses the \SI{100}{\giga\hertz} threshold in the vertical architecture for
the first time through a U-shaped anode electrode. This electrode geometry reduces
$R_s$—and therefore $\tau_{\mathrm{RC}} = R_s C_j$ of \cref{eq:tau_rc}—by
approximately \SI{36}{\percent} relative to a straight-electrode design, while
simultaneously delivering $\mathcal{R} = \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}$ and
$I_d = \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}$ at \SI{-1}{\volt}. Broader reviews of the design
space are provided by Wang~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Wang2024} and Yan~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Yan2024},
and the multiphysics modelling context is extensively treated in the
doctoral dissertation of Alasio~\cite{Alasio2024}.

provided by a \SI{40}{\micro\metre} adiabatic taper, designed to expand the
fundamental TE mode from the \SI{500}{\nano\metre} Si wire into the wider Ge mesa
with a coupling efficiency $\eta_c$ approaching unity, as verified by the FDTD
simulation of \cref{subsec:pd_fdtd}.

\subsection{Optical Simulation: Three-Dimensional FDTD}
\label{subsec:pd_fdtd}

The optical simulation solves the time-domain Maxwell's equations of
\cref{eq:maxwell_curl_e,eq:maxwell_curl_h} throughout a computational domain
that encompasses the silicon input waveguide, the \SI{40}{\micro\metre} adiabatic
taper, the complete germanium absorber, and the surrounding silicon dioxide cladding.
The solver (Lumerical FDTD Solutions 2020~R2) discretises the continuous electromagnetic
field on a Yee staggered grid, advancing the field components in time using the
second-order Courant-stable leapfrog scheme. The complex permittivity
$\tilde{\varepsilon}(\omega) = \varepsilon_0\tilde{n}^2(\omega)$ is sampled at
the material-dependent optical frequency through the tabulated complex refractive
indices of \cref{eq:complex_n}; for germanium at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre},
$n \approx 4.27$ and $\kappa \approx 0.22$, yielding
$\alpha \approx \SI{2.1e3}{\per\centi\metre}$ from \cref{eq:alpha_kappa}, consistent
with published Ge optical constants.

A broadband fundamental TE mode source is launched at the waveguide input plane.
Frequency-domain power monitors at the output facet and all domain boundaries record
the complex transmission $T(\omega)$ and reflection $R(\omega)$ coefficients, from
which the spectrally resolved absorption fraction is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}(\omega) = 1 - |T(\omega)|^2 - |R(\omega)|^2
    - \sum_i |T_{\mathrm{sub},i}(\omega)|^2,
    \label{eq:fdtd_abs}
\end{equation}
where the sum accounts for power leaking into substrate modes. A \SI{20}{\nano\metre}
isotropic mesh override within the germanium region resolves the exponential
generation-rate gradient of \cref{eq:gen_rate}; the mesh size was determined by
convergence testing at \SI{10}{\nano\metre} and \SI{20}{\nano\metre}, with less than
\SI{0.5}{\percent} deviation in $\mathcal{A}$. Perfectly matched layer boundary
conditions are applied at all domain faces to suppress artificial reflections.

The absorber length is swept from \SIrange{2}{8}{\micro\metre} to identify the
saturation point of $\mathcal{A}(L)$ from \cref{eq:abs_fraction}. The three-dimensional
optical generation rate map $G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r})$, computed from
\cref{eq:gen_rate} using the absorbed power density extracted by the FDTD solver,
is written to the file \texttt{ge\_gen\_oband.mat} and imported directly into the
CHARGE solver as the spatially distributed source term of
\cref{eq:cont_n_transient,eq:cont_p_transient}.

\subsection{Electrical Simulation: Three-Dimensional Drift-Diffusion}
\label{subsec:pd_charge}

Carrier transport is simulated in Lumerical DEVICE CHARGE under three-dimensional
steady-state conditions, solving the PDE system of
\cref{eq:poisson,eq:Jn,eq:Jp,eq:cont_n,eq:cont_p} on an adaptive finite-element
mesh. The mesh is refined at material interfaces and depletion boundaries according
to a curvature-based criterion, ensuring resolution of the steep carrier density
gradients at the $n^{++}/i$ and $i/p^{++}$ junctions. Carrier mobilities in
germanium follow a velocity-saturation model of the form:
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{n,p}(|\mathbf{E}|) = \frac{\mu_{n,p}^{(0)}}
    {\left[1 + \left(\mu_{n,p}^{(0)}|\mathbf{E}|/v_{\mathrm{d,sat},{n,h}}\right)^{\beta}\right]^{1/\beta}},
    \label{eq:mobility_sat}
\end{equation}
which recovers $v_{\mathrm{d,sat},h} = \SI{4e6}{\centi\metre\per\second}$ and
$v_{\mathrm{d,sat},e} = \SI{6e6}{\centi\metre\per\second}$ at high fields. SRH
recombination is activated in the germanium absorber with calibrated lifetimes
$\tau_n = \tau_p = \SI{5}{\nano\second}$, chosen so that \cref{eq:jdark_srh,eq:idark_total}
reproduce the experimental dark current of \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere} at \SI{-1}{\volt}.
Auger recombination is enabled in the heavily doped contact layers, and the interface
recombination velocity $v_s$ is applied at the Ge/Si heterojunction to model the
$J_{\mathrm{surf}}$ term of \cref{eq:jdark_surf}. Ohmic boundary conditions are
imposed at the cathode (biased to the desired reverse voltage $V < 0$) and anode
contacts (grounded, $V = 0$).

All performance metrics are extracted directly from the CHARGE solution: $\mathcal{R}$
from \cref{eq:responsivity} using the photocurrent at the cathode contact;
$\eta_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{ext}}$ from \cref{eq:iqe,eq:eqe};
$C_j$ from the small-signal (AC) analysis at zero perturbation frequency; and
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ from \cref{eq:bw_total} using the extracted $C_j$ and
modelled $R_s$ from \cref{eq:rs_anode}.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Networks as a Complementary Verification Solver}
\label{subsec:pd_pinns}

As an independent cross-validation tool, a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN)
is employed to solve the drift-diffusion PDE system of
\cref{eq:poisson,eq:Jn,eq:Jp,eq:cont_n,eq:cont_p} without a spatial mesh.
A PINN approximates the solution fields $u = \{n, p, \phi\}$ by a deep neural
network $\hat{u}(\mathbf{r};\boldsymbol{\theta})$ with parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$,
trained to minimise the composite loss functional:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
    = \lambda_{\mathrm{PDE}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PDE}}
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}},
    \label{eq:pinn_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{PDE}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{data}} > 0$
are weighting hyperparameters balancing the three contributions. The PDE residual
loss is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PDE}}
    = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}
    \Bigl[
      \bigl|\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\nabla\hat{\phi}) + q(\hat{p} - \hat{n}
            + N_D^+ - N_A^-)\bigr|^2
    + \bigl|\tfrac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_n
            - U_{\mathrm{net}}(\hat{n},\hat{p}) + G_{\mathrm{opt}}\bigr|^2
    + \bigl|\tfrac{1}{q}\nabla\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_p
            + U_{\mathrm{net}}(\hat{n},\hat{p}) - G_{\mathrm{opt}}\bigr|^2
    \Bigr]_{\mathbf{r}_i},
    \label{eq:pinn_pde_loss}
\end{equation}
evaluated at $N_c$ collocation points $\{\mathbf{r}_i\}$ sampled by quasi-random
(Sobol) sequences throughout the device domain $\Omega$. The key structural
distinction from finite-element discretisation is that all spatial derivatives
in \cref{eq:pinn_pde_loss}—including the second-order terms in the Poisson
equation—are computed via \emph{reverse-mode automatic differentiation} through the
network's computational graph, eliminating the requirement for a structured mesh
and the associated approximation error from spatial discretisation. The boundary
condition loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BC}}$ enforces Ohmic contact conditions at
the metal electrodes (Dirichlet conditions on $\hat{\phi}$, $\hat{n}$, $\hat{p}$
set by the equilibrium contact doping) and current continuity at material interfaces.
The optional data term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}$ anchors the network solution
to a sparse set of measurement points, providing a regularisation mechanism when
experimental data are available.

At selected bias voltages ($V = 0, -0.5, -1.0$\,V), the PINN and CHARGE solutions
for the band diagram, carrier density profiles, and electric field magnitude are
compared. Agreement between the two independently formulated solvers—one based on
standard Galerkin finite-element discretisation and the other on collocation-based
neural network optimisation—provides the validation assurance that neither solution
is an artefact of its particular numerical implementation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:pd_discussion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The results of \cref{sec:pd_results} are physically interpretable through the
device-physics framework of \cref{sec:pd_concept} and agree quantitatively with
the experimental benchmark of \cref{tab:pd_literature}. The following paragraphs
provide a causal, equation-grounded interpretation of each result and identify
the active design trade-offs and residual limiting factors.

\paragraph{Absorption profile and the optical-to-electrical coupling chain.}
The exponential spatial decay of $G_{\mathrm{opt}}(\mathbf{r})$ in
\cref{fig:gen_rate_map} is a direct consequence of Beer-Lambert attenuation
at the Ge absorption coefficient derived from \cref{eq:alpha_kappa}. The
saturation of $\mathcal{A}$ beyond \SI{6}{\micro\metre} establishes the design
point at which further length increase does not improve EQE through \cref{eq:qe_chain}
but does increase $C_j \propto A_j \propto L$ through \cref{eq:cj}. The near-unity
IQE of $\geq\SI{99}{\percent}$, extracted from \cref{eq:iqe}, confirms that the
electric field of \cref{fig:efield} is sufficient to sweep photogenerated carriers
to their respective contacts before SRH recombination occurs with the calibrated
lifetime $\tau_0 = \SI{5}{\nano\second}$. The residual gap between IQE and EQE,
quantified through \cref{eq:qe_chain} as a coupling efficiency $\eta_c < 1$,
is entirely attributable to optical reflection and mode mismatch at the taper entry
face; it could in principle be reduced by further optimising the taper profile
or by incorporating an anti-reflection layer at the Si/Ge interface.

\paragraph{Dark current mechanisms and temperature sensitivity.}
The \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere} dark current is dominated by SRH generation in the
intrinsic germanium layer. Substituting $n_i = \SI{2.4e13}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$,
$W_i = \SI{350}{\nano\metre}$, and $\tau_0 = \SI{5}{\nano\second}$ into
\cref{eq:jdark_srh} yields
$J_{\mathrm{SRH}} \approx \SI{27}{\milli\ampere\per\metre\squared}$,
which reproduces the simulated $I_d$ for the device junction area
$A_j = \SI{6}{\micro\metre\squared}$. The diffusion component from
\cref{eq:jdark_diff} is suppressed by the heavy doping of the contact layers:
for $N_D = \SI{e19}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ and $N_A = \SI{e20}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$,
the minority carrier equilibrium densities $p_{n0} = n_i^2/N_D \approx \SI{5.8e7}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$
and $n_{p0} = n_i^2/N_A \approx \SI{5.8e6}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ are negligible.
The strong exponential temperature dependence of $n_i \propto \exp(-E_g/2kT)$
in germanium—where $E_{g,\mathrm{ind}} = \SI{0.66}{\electronvolt}$ is substantially
smaller than silicon's \SI{1.12}{\electronvolt}—implies that $I_d$ will approximately
double for every $\Delta T \approx \SI{10}{\kelvin}$ increase in junction temperature,
a sensitivity that must be accounted for in the thermal management budget of any
high-density photonic integration platform. From \cref{eq:shot}, the dark condition
shot noise spectral density is $S_{\mathrm{shot}} = 2qI_d \approx
\SI{4.2e-28}{\ampere\squared\per\hertz}$, an irreducible noise contribution that
feeds directly into the receiver sensitivity expression derived in
\cref{sec:pd_system}.

\paragraph{Bandwidth and the intrinsic-layer thickness trade-off.}
The intrinsic thickness $W_i = \SI{350}{\nano\metre}$ is positioned in the regime
where both transit-time and RC contributions to $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ are significant.
The transit-time limit $f_{\mathrm{tt}} \approx \SI{51}{\giga\hertz}$ from
\cref{eq:bw_tt} is confirmed as the more restrictive bandwidth contribution at this
operating point, meaning that the U-shaped electrode has successfully elevated
$f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ above $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$. Any attempt to improve $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$
further by reducing $W_i$ would simultaneously decrease $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$ through
\cref{eq:bw_tt} while increasing the electric field for a given bias, elevating dark
current through field-enhanced SRH. The only remaining lever for bandwidth improvement
in the vertical architecture is therefore a further reduction of $R_s$ below the
value achieved by the U-shaped electrode, which would push $f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ to the
point where transit-time saturation from \cref{eq:bw_tt} is reached and no further
electrical improvement is possible. At that fundamental limit, only a reduction of
$W_i$ (with the accompanying dark current penalty) or a migration to the lateral
junction geometry would yield additional bandwidth.

\paragraph{Comparison with the literature and architectural constraints.}
The simulated metrics are in quantitative agreement with the experimental
benchmark~\cite{Shi2024} and are consistent with the broader literature of
\cref{tab:pd_literature}. The \SI{265}{\giga\hertz} bandwidth of the lateral junction
device of Lischke~\textit{et al.}~\cite{Lischke2021} remains structurally inaccessible
to the vertical architecture at the same $W_i$, because in the lateral geometry
$C_j$ is controlled by the horizontal depletion width independently of the optical
mode depth, enabling $A_j$ to be reduced below the minimum value imposed by the
waveguide-coupled absorption area. In the vertical architecture, $A_j = W_m L$ is
simultaneously constrained from below by the need for $\mathcal{A} \approx 1$
through \cref{eq:abs_fraction} (requiring $L \geq \alpha^{-1} \ln[1/(1-\mathcal{A})]$)
and from the RC analysis through the requirement that $f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ not degrade
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$. The present simulation framework quantitatively locates the
operating point within these competing constraints and identifies $R_s C_j$ as the
quantity that further electrode miniaturisation must address.

\section{Device-to-System Projection}
\label{sec:pd_system}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The performance metrics extracted in \cref{sec:pd_results} are not isolated device
quantities: they enter directly into the sensitivity budget of the optical receiver
chain. This section derives, strictly from the device-level results and without
introducing independent system theory, the first-order implication of the simulated
responsivity and bandwidth for the minimum detectable optical power at the input
facet of the silicon photonic chip. The derivation provides the quantitative bridge
to the digital signal processing chapters of this thesis, which adopt the resulting
receiver sensitivity as an input parameter rather than deriving it independently.

\subsection{Shot-Noise-Limited Receiver Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:pd_sens}

In the shot-noise-limited regime, where the dark current shot noise $2qI_d$ and the
signal shot noise $2qI_{\mathrm{ph}}$ dominate over thermal noise from $R_L$,
the signal-to-noise ratio at the output of the detector in a detection bandwidth
$B_e = f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{SNR}
    = \frac{I_{\mathrm{ph}}^2}{2q(I_d + I_{\mathrm{ph}})\,B_e}
    = \frac{(\mathcal{R}\,P_{\mathrm{opt}})^2}{2q(\mathcal{R}\,P_{\mathrm{opt}} + I_d)\,B_e}.
    \label{eq:snr_shot}
\end{equation}
Setting $\mathrm{SNR} = Q^2$, where $Q$ is the Q-factor corresponding to the
target bit-error rate ($Q \approx 7.03$ for BER $= 10^{-12}$ in OOK), and solving
\cref{eq:snr_shot} for $P_{\mathrm{opt}}$ gives the minimum detectable average power,
or \emph{sensitivity}:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{sens}}
    = \frac{Q^2 q B_e}{\mathcal{R}}
      + \frac{Q}{\mathcal{R}}\sqrt{Q^2 q^2 B_e^2 + 2q I_d B_e}.
    \label{eq:sens_full}
\end{equation}
In the limit $I_d \ll \mathcal{R}\,P_{\mathrm{sens}}$ (signal-dominated shot noise),
\cref{eq:sens_full} reduces to:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{sens}} \approx \frac{2Q^2 q B_e}{\mathcal{R}}
    \quad \text{(signal-dominated)},
    \label{eq:sens_signal}
\end{equation}
while in the limit $I_d \gg \mathcal{R}\,P_{\mathrm{sens}}$ (dark-current-dominated):
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{sens}} \approx \frac{Q}{\mathcal{R}}\sqrt{2qI_d B_e}
    \quad \text{(dark-current-dominated)}.
    \label{eq:sens_dark}
\end{equation}
Substituting the simulated values $\mathcal{R} = \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}$,
$I_d = \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}$, and $B_e = f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$
into \cref{eq:sens_full} at $Q = 7.03$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{sens}} \approx \SI{-17.1}{\dBm}.
    \label{eq:sens_result}
\end{equation}
This value constitutes the \emph{device-imposed lower bound} on the optical launch
power required at the receiver input for error-free detection at \SI{103}{\giga\baud}.
The full receiver sensitivity, after accounting for transimpedance amplifier noise
and DSP overhead, is analysed in the system chapter; the present derivation
establishes the photodetector contribution to that budget as
$P_{\mathrm{sens}} \approx \SI{-17.1}{\dBm}$ in the ideal shot-noise-limited case.

\subsection{Bandwidth and Modulation Format Implications}
\label{subsec:pd_format}

The derived $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} \geq \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$ is sufficient to support
symbol rates up to approximately \SI{100}{\giga\baud} in OOK with less than
\SI{1}{\dB} of bandwidth-induced signal roll-off at the Nyquist frequency, using
the standard approximation $f_{\mathrm{Nyq}} \leq 0.7\,f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ for
the usable passband of a single-pole roll-off model~\cite{SalehTeich1991}. For
four-level pulse-amplitude modulation (PAM-4), which encodes two bits per symbol
and therefore requires only half the symbol rate for a given bit rate, the same
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ supports up to approximately \SI{200}{\giga\bit\per\second}
with reduced bandwidth penalty. The DSP-layer equalisation required to compensate
the residual roll-off of the photodetector transfer function in the range
$0.7\,f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} < f < f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$ is addressed in the companion
digital signal processing chapter; the photodetector model developed in the present
chapter—a first-order low-pass transfer function with $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} =
\SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$ and flat-band responsivity $\mathcal{R} = \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}$—
provides the input channel model for that equaliser design.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:pd_conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This chapter has presented a physically rigorous, simulation-driven characterisation
of a vertical n-i-p Ge-on-Si waveguide photodiode, constructed from first principles
in a manner that makes each design choice traceable to a specific equation or
physical argument. The concept review began with Maxwell's equations in a dispersive
medium, from which the complex refractive index $\tilde{n} = n + i\kappa$ was
defined and the absorption coefficient $\alpha = 4\pi\kappa/\lambda$ was derived
rather than asserted. The Beer-Lambert law, the carrier generation rate, the full
transient and steady-state drift-diffusion system, and all performance metrics were
then established in a consistent framework that served as the analytical backbone
for the entire subsequent analysis.

The three-dimensional FDTD and CHARGE simulation chain reproduced the full set of
benchmark metrics: $\mathcal{R} \geq \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}$,
$I_d = \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}$ at \SI{-1}{\volt}, and
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} \geq \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$. An IQE of $\geq\SI{99}{\percent}$
confirmed that carrier collection within the active volume is nearly ideal, with the
residual EQE shortfall arising exclusively from optical coupling loss at the taper
rather than from recombination. The PINN surrogate independently reproduced the
CHARGE band diagram, carrier density, and electric field profiles at selected bias
points through automatic differentiation of the PDE residuals of
\cref{eq:pinn_pde_loss}, providing cross-validation at the level of the governing
equations rather than merely at the level of integral output quantities.

Three principal physical insights emerge from this study. First, the absorption
coefficient of germanium at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre} is a direct consequence of
its band structure—specifically, the proximity of the $\Gamma$-point direct gap
($E_{g,\mathrm{dir}} = \SI{0.80}{\electronvolt}$) to the O-band photon energy
(\SI{0.946}{\electronvolt})—and is derivable from the imaginary part of the
complex refractive index through \cref{eq:alpha_kappa}. Second, the bandwidth of
the vertical architecture is governed by $\tau_{\mathrm{RC}} = R_s C_j$ of
\cref{eq:tau_rc} once the absorber thickness is fixed by the simultaneous requirements
of \cref{eq:abs_fraction} and \cref{eq:bw_tt}; the U-shaped electrode is the decisive
engineering contribution enabling the \SI{103}{\giga\hertz} result, as it reduces
$R_s$ without modifying any parameter that affects $f_{\mathrm{tt}}$, $C_j$, or
the optical mode profile. Third, the dark current is irreducibly dominated by SRH
generation in the intrinsic germanium, as quantified by \cref{eq:jdark_srh}, and
its magnitude is set by the combination of $n_i$, $W_i$, and the material-dependent
lifetime $\tau_0$, none of which can be independently optimised without affecting
another performance metric.

The device-to-system projection of \cref{sec:pd_system} established that these
device metrics translate into a shot-noise-limited receiver sensitivity of
approximately \SI{-17.1}{\dBm} at \SI{103}{\giga\baud}, providing the quantitative
boundary condition that the transimpedance amplifier and DSP equaliser must satisfy
in the system architecture developed in subsequent chapters.

Future experimental work should address three specific open questions: the direct
measurement of $\tau_0$ in as-grown Ge-on-Si epitaxial layers through time-resolved
photoluminescence, to validate or refine the calibrated SRH lifetime assumption;
the extraction of the parasitic bondpad capacitance $C_{\mathrm{par}}$ from on-wafer
S-parameter measurements, to separate it from $C_j$ in the bandwidth model and
enable a quantitative prediction of $f_{\mathrm{RC}}$ without relying on the
modelled $R_s$; and the exploration of modified electrode topologies—in particular,
wrap-around cathode contacts on the top $n^{++}$-Ge surface—that might further
reduce $R_s$ toward the transit-time bandwidth floor set by \cref{eq:bw_tt}.
