\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:pd_intro}

High-speed optical receivers for intra-datacenter PAM-4 links require photodetectors
with simultaneous high responsivity, low dark current, and bandwidth exceeding
\SI{100}{\giga\hertz}. Germanium-on-silicon (Ge-on-Si) vertical n-i-p photodiodes
satisfy all three constraints within standard SOI fabrication~\cite{Shi:24}.
This chapter presents the full design and simulation of such a device targeting the
O-band (\SI{1310}{\nano\metre}) with performance benchmarks drawn from~\cite{Shi:24}.

\section{Device Architecture}
\label{sec:pd_architecture}

\subsection{Layer Stack}
The device is a vertical n-i-p structure epitaxially grown on a
\SI{220}{\nano\metre} SOI wafer:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Device layer stack parameters.}
\label{tab:layer_stack}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Layer & Material & Thickness & Doping \\
\midrule
Cathode contact & $n^{++}$-Ge & \SI{50}{\nano\metre}  & $N_D = \SI{e19}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ \\
Absorber        & $i$-Ge      & \SI{350}{\nano\metre} & $N_D = \SI{e13}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ \\
Platform        & $p^{++}$-Si & \SI{220}{\nano\metre} & $N_A = \SI{e20}{\per\centi\metre\cubed}$ \\
BOX             & \ce{SiO2}   & \SI{2}{\micro\metre}  & — \\
Substrate       & Si          & —                     & — \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Electrode Geometry}
Anode contacts are made to the $p^{++}$-Si platform via a U-shaped metal electrode
surrounding the Ge mesa (arms at $y = \pm\SI{2.5}{\micro\metre}$ from centre, back
closure at $x = L_{\mathrm{Ge}}$). This geometry minimises junction capacitance while
keeping series resistance low, yielding an RC-limited bandwidth of approximately
\SI{140}{\giga\hertz} with $R_S = \SI{12.9}{\ohm}$ and $C_j = \SI{22.6}{\femto\farad}$.

\section{FDTD Optical Simulation}
\label{sec:pd_fdtd}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}
Three-dimensional finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations were performed using
Lumerical FDTD Solutions 2020~R2. The simulation domain spans the Si input waveguide,
a \SI{40}{\micro\metre} adiabatic spot-size converter (taper: \SI{500}{\nano\metre}
to \SI{5}{\micro\metre} in width), and the full Ge absorber length $L_{\mathrm{Ge}}$.
A perfectly matched layer (PML) terminates all boundaries except $y_{\min}$, which
exploits the device's lateral symmetry (Symmetric BC). Mesh resolution within the
Ge region is \SI{20}{\nano\metre} isotropic.

The fundamental TE mode of the input waveguide is launched from a mode source and
evanescently couples into the Ge layer via the taper. Power monitors record
transmission ($T$) and back-reflection ($R$); absorption is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    A = 1 - |T| - |R|
    \label{eq:absorption}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ge Length Optimisation}
\label{sec:ge_length}
The Ge mesa length $L_{\mathrm{Ge}}$ was swept from \SIrange{2}{8}{\micro\metre}
to find the minimum length achieving $A \geq 95\%$ at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre}.
Results are plotted in \cref{fig:absorption_vs_length}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/absorption_vs_Ge_length}
    \caption{Optical absorption at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre} vs Ge mesa length for
             fundamental TE excitation. The optimal length is reached at
             $A \geq 95\%$.}
    \label{fig:absorption_vs_length}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Polarisation Dependence}
The polarisation-dependent loss (PDL) between TE and TM fundamental modes was
evaluated at the optimal $L_{\mathrm{Ge}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{PDL} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{A_{\mathrm{TE}}}{A_{\mathrm{TM}}}\right)
    \label{eq:pdl}
\end{equation}
The wide Ge mesa ($W_{\mathrm{Ge}} = \SI{5}{\micro\metre}$) ensures near-unity TM
absorption, giving $|\mathrm{PDL}| < \SI{0.05}{\decibel}$.

\subsection{Optical Generation Rate Export}
The volumetric optical generation rate $G(\mathbf{r})$ [\si{\per\metre\cubed\per\second}]
is derived from the absorbed power density monitored over the Ge region:
\begin{equation}
    G(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{r})}{h f}
    \label{eq:gen_rate}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is Planck's constant and $f = c/\lambda_c$. The 3-D array is exported to
\texttt{ge\_gen\_oband.mat} for import into the CHARGE solver (\cref{fig:gen_rate_map}).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/optical_generation_rate_map}
    \caption{2-D cross-section ($x$-$z$ plane) of the optical generation rate
             at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre}. Dashed white lines mark the Si/Ge and
             i-Ge/$n^{++}$ interfaces.}
    \label{fig:gen_rate_map}
\end{figure}

\section{CHARGE (Drift-Diffusion) Simulation}
\label{sec:pd_charge}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}
Carrier transport was simulated using Lumerical DEVICE CHARGE in 3-D steady-state
mode. Materials were configured with:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Silicon}: Palik optical constants; Masetti mobility model.
    \item \textbf{Germanium}: Palik constants; SRH recombination enabled with
          $\tau_n = \tau_p = \SI{5}{\nano\second}$ (calibrated to match
          $I_d = \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}$ at $V = \SI{-1}{\volt}$).
\end{itemize}

Doping regions and contact boundaries match \cref{tab:layer_stack}. A fine mesh
override (\SI{20}{\nano\metre} maximum edge length) covers the full Ge stack.
The cathode is swept from \SIrange{0}{4}{\volt} reverse bias in 17 points;
anode contacts are held at \SI{0}{\volt}.

\subsection{Dark Current}
The simulated dark I-V is shown in \cref{fig:iv_dark}. At $V = \SI{-1}{\volt}$:
\begin{equation}
    I_d = \SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}
    \label{eq:dark_current}
\end{equation}
consistent with the target from~\cite{Shi:24}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figures/dark_iv}
    \caption{Dark current vs reverse bias at \SI{300}{\kelvin}.}
    \label{fig:iv_dark}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Photocurrent and Responsivity}
Under \SI{100}{\micro\watt} optical input at \SI{1310}{\nano\metre}, the photocurrent
and external responsivity are:
\begin{align}
    I_{\mathrm{ph}} &= I_{\mathrm{ill}} - I_d                         \label{eq:photocurrent} \\
    \mathcal{R}     &= \frac{I_{\mathrm{ph}}}{P_{\mathrm{opt}}} \geq \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}
    \label{eq:responsivity}
\end{align}

\begin{figure}[H]

\end{figure}

\subsection{Bandwidth Estimation}
The 3\,dB bandwidth is limited by both carrier transit time and RC parasitics:
\begin{equation}
    f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1/f_{\mathrm{tt}}^2 + 1/f_{\mathrm{RC}}^2}}
    \geq \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}
    \label{eq:bandwidth}
\end{equation}
The modelled frequency response is shown in \cref{fig:freq_response}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering

    \caption{Normalised $S_{21}$ frequency response combining transit-time and RC
             transfer functions. The $-3$\,dB bandwidth exceeds \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}
             with the U-shaped electrode geometry.}
    \label{fig:freq_response}
\end{figure}

\section{System-Level PAM-4 Validation}
\label{sec:pd_system}

To verify the photodetector's suitability for high-speed link applications, a
MATLAB-based PAM-4 receiver model was constructed. The model includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item PAM-4 symbol generation at \SI{53.125}{\giga\baud}
          (106\,Gb/s IEEE 802.3bs-compliant rate).
    \item Root-raised-cosine pulse shaping with roll-off $\alpha = 0.35$.
    \item Quantum shot noise and thermal noise at $R_L = \SI{50}{\ohm}$.
    \item Bandwidth-limited detection through a $3^{\mathrm{rd}}$-order Butterworth
          low-pass filter matched to $f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}}$.
    \item Matched-filter detection with adaptive threshold estimation.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[H]

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:pd_conclusion}

A Ge-on-Si vertical n-i-p photodiode was designed and simulated using coupled
FDTD and drift-diffusion solvers, and validated at the system level with a PAM-4
receiver model. The design achieves the IEEE 802.3bs-compliant targets of
$\mathcal{R} \geq \SI{0.95}{\ampere\per\watt}$ and
$f_{3\,\mathrm{dB}} \geq \SI{103}{\giga\hertz}$ with a dark current of
\SI{1.3}{\nano\ampere}, confirming the simulation framework's accuracy against
published results~\cite{Shi:24}. The exported INTERCONNECT-ready compact model
enables direct insertion of this device into photonic link simulations.
All scripts and data are available at:
\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19652934}
